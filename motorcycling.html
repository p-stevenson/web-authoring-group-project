MOTORCYCLING

Less about speed than judgement, patience, and liking the road.

How to get started with Motorcycling?

Motorcycling looks exciting from the outside, but the day-to-day reality is more about attention than drama. Good riders spend most of their time reading traffic, noticing surface changes, checking mirrors, and keeping their head clear when things get busy.

If you are starting in Ireland, the UK, or elsewhere in Europe, the boring bits matter first: training, the right licence route, and proper kit. A smaller bike, a steady pre-ride check, and regular practice at slow-speed control will do more for your confidence than trying to look fearless.

Start Your Journey

Most riders build up in three fairly ordinary stages, even if the bikes get fancier later on.

1

BEGINNER

Start with the learner route that fits where you live: learner permit and IBT in Ireland, or provisional licence and CBT in the UK. Get decent CE-rated gear, choose a sensible first bike, and spend time on slow control, observation, lifesavers, and braking in low-risk spaces.

2

ENTHUSIAST

Once the basics stop feeling clumsy, the focus shifts to consistency. You start riding smoother lines, reading bends better, and checking the bike without having to remind yourself. Extra coaching or a BikeSafe day is usually money well spent.

3

EXPERT

Experienced riders are rarely the flashiest ones on the road. They plan ahead, manage fatigue and weather properly, and keep learning instead of assuming they have it cracked. A lot of them end up being the calm voice for newer riders as well.
